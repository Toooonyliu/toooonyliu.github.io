Human × AI / A design process reflection

AI made it tangible.
I had to make it matter.

The most important change was deciding what to build.
Four phases, real feedback, and a pivotal rethink at the PRD stage.

01 / Discover

Features are not the whole story.

AI helped organize the research. I pushed it toward a harder question: why would someone use Flighty when they already have an airline app?

WHAT WORKED

A faster starting point

Product information, competitor comparisons and public reviews gave us material to investigate.

WHAT NEEDED MY JUDGMENT

A feature list was not enough

I had to ask for evidence of different user needs. Public reviews still cannot substitute for interviews.

FROM OUR CONVERSATIONRecorded feedback
“we also add a competitive anlysis with data back-up proving why user choose to use flighty is different than user choose to use airline app.”

02 / Define

The moment we questioned the idea.

The real pivot happened at the PRD stage. We had settled on Trips. A challenge from my professor made us reconsider whether the opportunity was valuable enough.

  1. INITIAL DIRECTIONTripsA journey-focused concept
  2. HUMAN CHALLENGEWhy build it?Professor feedback prompted a rethink
  3. REASSESSMENTUser + business valueRevisit the opportunity, not just the UI
  4. REVISED DIRECTIONTrips + MileageReassess trip value; add practical flight decisions

Useful after the challenge

03 / Develop

A familiar pattern. The wrong context.

Prototypes made the ideas easy to question. A yearly flight chart belonged in an all-time passport, but it did little to explain a specific trip.

QUESTIONED PATTERNFlights per yearUseful for a lifetime of flying
→
REVISED TRIP VIEWDeparture → ArrivalUseful for understanding this journey
WHAT WORKED

Something concrete to critique

We could turn feedback into revised screens: compact accounts, trip summaries and projected mileage growth.

WHAT NEEDED MY JUDGMENT

Patterns needed judgment

Reusing the passport chart did not make it useful. I redirected the view toward the start and finish of a journey.

04 / Deliver

Working is not the same as valuable.

AI helped implement and check the demo. I kept asking for clearer benefits, better hierarchy and policy evidence. User value still needs evaluation with frequent flyers.

WHAT WORKED

Fast implementation and checks

The published iteration passed 53 automated tests and desktop/mobile walkthroughs. This supported functional confidence.

WHAT NEEDED MY JUDGMENT

Technical checks had a limit

They did not prove usability or business impact. Fares and balances remain scenarios; benefit eligibility needs conditions.

FROM OUR CONVERSATIONRecorded feedback
“Make a clear visual hierachy on mileage benefit, with real information from each airline's policy”

Tony · request for clearer, verified mileage benefits

Read the supporting excerpt
“what will prove the value of these two function and how can we improve the design?”

WHAT I LEARNED

Separate “it works” from “it helps.” Test both.
