Human × AI / A design process reflection

AI made it tangible.
I had to make it matter.

The most important change was deciding what to build.
Four phases, real feedback, and a pivotal rethink at the PRD stage.

01 / Discover

Features are not the whole story.

Why choose Flighty over an airline app? I asked AI to investigate the user need behind the features.

WHAT WORKED

A faster starting point

AI organized product information, competitors and public reviews.

WHERE I STEPPED IN

A feature list was not enough

I pushed for evidence of user needs. Reviews alone were not enough.

THE CONVERSATION

Researching the difference

From our competitive-research discussion

  1. TL
    Tony

    we also add a competitive anlysis with data back-up proving why user choose to use flighty is different than user choose to use airline app.

    even go to app store and pull down some comments about what really make flighty competitive

  2. AI
    AI

    The competitive argument should be about different jobs, not “Flighty has better features.”

    Ratings therefore do not establish Flighty’s superiority; the specific tasks reviewers describe are more useful evidence.

More from the AI response
  1. AI
    AI

    We should also briefly acknowledge specialist competition: AwardWallet already aggregates rewards and travel plans. Multi-program coverage alone is not unique.

What changedThe comparison shifted from feature counts to different traveler needs.

03 / Develop

A familiar pattern. The wrong context.

A yearly chart made sense for a lifetime of flights. For a single trip, I needed departure and arrival.

QUESTIONED PATTERNFlights per yearUseful for a lifetime of flying
→
REVISED TRIP VIEWDeparture → ArrivalUseful for understanding this journey
WHAT WORKED

Something concrete to critique

Working screens made it easier to spot problems and try changes.

WHERE I STEPPED IN

Patterns needed judgment

I questioned the reused chart and redirected it toward the journey.

THE CONVERSATION

Questioning a familiar chart

From our trip-passport feedback and review

    Tony

04 / Deliver

Working is not the same as valuable.

The demo worked. Next, I asked whether the benefits were clear, accurate and useful.

WHAT WORKED

Fast implementation and checks

AI helped build and check the demo: 53 tests plus desktop/mobile walkthroughs.

WHERE I STEPPED IN

Technical checks had a limit

Functional checks did not prove user value. Real-world evaluation is still needed.

THE CONVERSATION

Making benefits specific

From our mileage-benefits revision

  1. TL
    Tony

    Make a clear visual hierachy on mileage benefit, with real information from each airline's policy

    what will prove the value of these two function and how can we improve the design?

  2. AI
    AI

    The policy review found a useful distinction to make prominent: a tier benefit may apply only to certain flights or require an extra step.

    I’ll separate “Available now,” “Conditions apply,” and “Next tier” so the badges don’t overpromise.

More from the AI response
  1. AI
    AI

    Both could encourage repeat use between flights, but that is a hypothesis—not demonstrated business impact.

What changedBenefits gained conditions and sources. Customer value remains a hypothesis to test.

Selected excerpts, original wording. Paragraphs may omit intervening text. Source notes ↗

WHAT I LEARNED

Separate “it works” from “it helps.” Test both.
