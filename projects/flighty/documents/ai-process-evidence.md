# Human–AI process evidence

Real user and assistant excerpts verified against the project conversation record. Original spelling and wording retained; Markdown emphasis removed for display. Paragraphs are separate selections and may omit intervening text. The cards are excerpts from related exchanges, not complete unabridged transcripts. “What changed” is editorial interpretation.

## discover

From our competitive-research discussion.

### Tony

> we also add a competitive anlysis with data back-up proving why user choose to use flighty is different than user choose to use airline app.

> even go to app store and pull down some comments about what really make flighty competitive

### AI

> The competitive argument should be about different jobs, not “Flighty has better features.”

> Ratings therefore do not establish Flighty’s superiority; the specific tasks reviewers describe are more useful evidence.

### Additional excerpt — AI

> We should also briefly acknowledge specialist competition: AwardWallet already aggregates rewards and travel plans. Multi-program coverage alone is not unique.

Editorial summary: The comparison shifted from feature counts to different traveler needs.

## develop

From our trip-passport feedback and review.

### Tony

> its not very useful information to show flights per year

> maybe instead show when each airline take off and landed, like a reminder of when the trip start and finish

### AI

> The screens confirm both layout issues: one mileage account fills most of the home panel, and Chicago’s Trip Passport uses a large chart to show just “2 flights in 2026.”

### Additional excerpt — AI

> One important detail for the planner: the demo stores balances and goals, but not the traveler’s confirmed current tier or its validity dates. We’ll need those to show benefits accurately.

Editorial summary: Departure, arrival and flight legs took priority over the yearly chart.

## deliver

From our mileage-benefits revision.

### Tony

> Make a clear visual hierachy on mileage benefit, with real information from each airline's policy

> what will prove the value of these two function and how can we improve the design?

### AI

> The policy review found a useful distinction to make prominent: a tier benefit may apply only to certain flights or require an extra step.

> I’ll separate “Available now,” “Conditions apply,” and “Next tier” so the badges don’t overpromise.

### Additional excerpt — AI

> Both could encourage repeat use between flights, but that is a hypothesis—not demonstrated business impact.

Editorial summary: Benefits gained conditions and sources. Customer value remains a hypothesis to test.
